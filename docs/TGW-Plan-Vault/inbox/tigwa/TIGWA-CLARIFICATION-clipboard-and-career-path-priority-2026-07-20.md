## Direction

Dave identifies two likely starting points for later validation:

1. A clipboard/event-transfer tool that works within Android’s acceptable permission model.
2. A high-value employment/consulting path centered on this kind of governed-agent, evidence, recovery, and state-machine work.

## Clipboard hypothesis

The potential wedge is not generic clipboard synchronization. It is a system that satisfies a real Android-permission-constrained transfer need without ambient clipboard capture, background harvesting, broad accessibility abuse, or opaque cloud mirroring.

Dave reports a specific, real platform constraint: beginning around Android 12, apps that access the clipboard require the operator to re-authorize that access after every device reboot. The exact Android version, permission/API mechanism, device/OEM variation, Play-distribution implications, and any compliant alternative are future verification targets; the report is retained as operator evidence rather than generalized platform fact until that review.

Future discovery must establish:

- the reported per-reboot authorization behavior and its actual Android permission/lifecycle mechanism by supported OS version, device/OEM, and distribution channel;
- what existing tools require, fail at, or make users uncomfortable with;
- one concrete user workflow where explicit selection, named destination/device, receipt, expiry, and local insertion materially improve the experience;
- whether the safest design can still be sufficiently frictionless to earn adoption;
- self-hosted versus hosted-service trust, support, and cost boundaries.

## Career hypothesis

The marketable capability is a product/operations discipline for making AI-agent systems governable: deterministic transitions, human gates, provenance, anomaly visibility, bounded authority, and rebuild/recovery evidence. Any later brief must use sanitized, non-sensitive examples and must not expose TGW data, private architecture, credentials, or unreviewed claims.
